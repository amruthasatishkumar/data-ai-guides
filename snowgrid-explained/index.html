Snowgrid, explained

What Snowgrid is, how it works underneath, and how its cross-cloud replication features can move a Snowflake account from AWS to Azure.

1. The short version

The three things to know.

What it is

A name for the cross-cloud layer

Snowflake describes Snowgrid as its cross-region, cross-cloud technology layer. It is how separate Snowflake accounts, in different regions and on AWS, Azure or Google Cloud, are connected so they can share data, apply governance and fail over to each other.

What it is not

Not a product you install

There is no "Snowgrid" account type or switch. You use its features, such as replication groups, failover groups, connections and listings, between accounts in the same Snowflake organization.

Why it matters here

DR tooling doubles as migration tooling

Snowflake's own docs list "Migration" as a use case: move your account to a different region or cloud platform without disrupting your business. That is exactly AWS to Azure.

In one line: your Snowflake stays Snowflake. Its built-in cross-cloud replication copies the account to Azure, you test it side by side, then point your users at the Azure copy.

2. The mental model

A Snowflake organization is a set of Snowflake accounts, each living in one cloud region. For your migration only two matter: the AWS account you have today (source) and a new Azure account (target). Snowgrid is the road between them.

Your migration, in one picture

The general Snowgrid picture (context)

Snowgrid can connect more than two accounts, including Google Cloud, and is also used for sharing. Your migration uses only the AWS and Azure pair above.

Snowflake frames Snowgrid in three pillars. One drives your migration.

Context only

Cross-cloud collaboration (sharing, listings)

Share and consume data across regions and clouds without building ETL. Features: secure data sharing, listings, Cross-Cloud Auto-Fulfillment.

Verify only

Cross-cloud governance

Policies, tags and lineage follow the data so rules apply consistently wherever it lives.

Your migration

Business continuity (replication, failover, Client Redirect)

Replicate more than data (accounts, users, roles, warehouses) and fail over across clouds. Features: replication and failover groups, Client Redirect.

For the migration, business continuity does the heavy lifting: replication and failover groups move the data and account objects, and Client Redirect moves the clients. Collaboration matters only if you publish data to others. Governance is something you verify on the Azure account, not something you build.

3. The building blocks

Eight concepts. Blocks 1 to 5 are the migration itself. Blocks 6 to 8 are supporting: relevant only in some situations, or checks to run rather than things to build. Each card says what it is, how it works, what it does in the migration, and what to watch for.

1

Organization and accounts

Core to migration
What it is
An organization is the umbrella over all of your Snowflake accounts. Each account lives in exactly one region on one cloud.
How it works
Replication and failover only work between accounts in the same organization. An organization administrator must first enable replication on each source and target account.
In the migration
Create a new account in the same organization, hosted on Azure. It becomes the target.
Watch for
The organization name is baked into connection URLs. Confirm it is what you want before creating connections. Changing it later needs Snowflake Support.
2

Replication group and failover group

Core to migration
What it is
A named bundle that says what to copy, to which accounts, and how often. A replication group copies. A failover group copies and can also be promoted to become the writable primary.
How it works
You list object types (for example USERS, ROLES, WAREHOUSES, RESOURCE MONITORS, DATABASES), allowed databases, allowed accounts and a schedule. Objects in one group are copied with point-in-time consistency.
In the migration
Use failover groups, since promotion is the cutover. You can split objects across several groups, for example to refresh some databases more often.
Watch for
Database replication and share replication work on all editions. Everything else (users, roles, warehouses, integrations and so on) needs Business Critical or higher.
3

Primary and secondary (read-only until promoted)

Core to migration
What it is
The group in the source account is the primary. Its copy in a target account is the secondary.
How it works
Replicated objects are read-only on the secondary. Users and roles must be created on the source and replicated, not edited on the target. On promotion, objects become writable on the new primary and read-only on the old one.
In the migration
The Azure account is a read-only mirror until you promote it. You can run validation queries on it.
Watch for
Warehouses replicate in a suspended state. Resume them on Azure and size them deliberately. Tell admins not to hand-create replicated object types on the Azure account.
4

Refresh and schedule

Core to migration
What it is
A refresh brings the secondary up to date with the primary. The first refresh copies everything. Later refreshes copy only changes.
How it works
Set a schedule such as every 10 minutes, or refresh manually. Replication is asynchronous, so the secondary lags the primary. Snowflake's docs say it is at most about twice the schedule interval behind during an outage.
In the migration
Keep a schedule running through validation so Azure stays close to current. Do a final refresh right before cutover.
Watch for
You cannot promote a group while a refresh is running. Suspend the group first. After failover, scheduled refreshes on the secondaries are suspended and must be resumed.
5

Connection and Client Redirect

Core to migration
What it is
A connection object gives you a stable URL of the form <organization>-<connection>.snowflakecomputing.com. It is not tied to one account.
How it works
The account holding the primary connection answers requests. Promoting a secondary connection in another account makes the same URL resolve there. Primary and secondary connections must be in different regions.
In the migration
Move every client (BI tools, apps, scripts) to the connection URL once, early. After that, cutover is an admin action, not a client change.
Watch for
If the account uses private connectivity (PrivateLink today, Azure Private Link after), you must manage a DNS CNAME record for the connection URL. Plan this with your network team.
6

Listings and Cross-Cloud Auto-Fulfillment

Only if you publish data
What it is
A listing offers a data product (a share or app package) to consumers. Auto-fulfillment automatically replicates that product to consumer regions, so the provider does not replicate by hand.
How it works
Snowflake manages a secure share area in each region and refreshes the product on the interval you set. Listings with auto-fulfillment can be added to a failover group.
In the migration
Only relevant if you publish data to others. Consumers keep getting the product while the provider account moves.
Watch for
Auto-fulfillment adds its own costs. Inbound shares (data other providers share with you) are not replicated and must be re-established.
7

Governance that travels

Verify during validation
What it is
Snowflake's positioning is that policies, tags and lineage follow data across clouds, so the same rules apply on Azure as on AWS.
How it works
Policies and tags attached to replicated objects replicate with them. Role grants replicate when roles are in scope. Network policies and parameters can replicate as well.
In the migration
You verify rather than rebuild: compare masking, row access and tag behavior on the secondary during validation.
Watch for
Policies that reference objects in a different group can break a refresh. Keep related objects in the same group and read Snowflake's replication considerations.
8

Encryption and security on the target

Supporting setup
What it is
Replicated data is protected in transit and at rest, and re-encrypted in the target account under its own key hierarchy.
How it works
Tri-Secret Secure is not enabled on the target by default. If you use it, enable it on the Azure account.
In the migration
Add "customer-managed key setup on Azure" to the build checklist if Tri-Secret Secure is in use.
Watch for
Identity (Entra ID SSO and SCIM) and Azure networking are separate setup on the Azure account. Replication carries supported user and integration objects, but test sign-in early.

5. What moves, what does not

Based on Snowflake's replication documentation. Always check the current version before committing to specifics.

Can replicate
  • Databases and the objects inside them, with data (all editions)
  • Outbound shares (all editions)
  • Users and their properties, roles and role hierarchies
  • Warehouses (arrive suspended), resource monitors
  • Network policies and parameters
  • Supported integrations
  • Listings with auto-fulfillment, external volumes for Iceberg
  • Cortex Search Services, Datasets, shared workspaces

Everything except databases and shares needs Business Critical or higher.

Does not replicate, or needs care
  • Inbound shares from other providers: not supported
  • External tables: not currently supported
  • Warehouse running state: not carried over
  • Resource monitor email settings for account administrators
  • Privileges on the groups themselves (REPLICATE, FAILOVER): grant on both sides
  • Cloud-specific plumbing: stages and storage integrations (S3 to ADLS), PrivateLink to Private Link, IAM to Entra ID

6. Cost model

From Snowflake's replication cost documentation. All charges are billed on the target account, which is the Azure account in this migration.

CostHow it works
Data transferInitial copy plus ongoing changes move between regions. The rate is determined by the location of the source account. See the Snowflake Service Consumption Table for current rates.
Replication computeSnowflake-provided compute works out the difference and copies data on each refresh. It appears as the REPLICATION service type in usage views.
Secondary storageStandard storage for the copy on Azure, plus background upkeep for materialized views and search optimization.
Failed refreshesStill charged. Data already copied can be reused by a later refresh within 14 days.
LeversCost is proportional to how much data changes and how often you refresh. Replicate only what you need, tune the schedule, and stop refreshing to stop the charges.
Estimate before you budget. Replication cost depends on your data size and daily change volume. Measure both with Snowflake's usage views, then price from the current Snowflake Service Consumption Table.

7. Things to confirm

What Snowflake's documentation confirms, and what to check for your own account before you start.

Confirmed in Snowflake docs
  • Snowgrid is Snowflake's cross-region, cross-cloud technology layer.
  • "Migration" is a listed use case for replication and failover: move an account to a different region or cloud platform.
  • Client Redirect docs mention minimizing application changes when migrating an account to another region or cloud.
  • Replication cost is billed on the target account. Transfer rate follows the source location.
  • Users, roles and other replicated objects are read-only on the target until promoted.
  • A group cannot be promoted while a refresh is in progress. Scheduled refreshes on secondaries are suspended after failover.
Check for your account
  • Client Redirect edition requirement: confirm in the replication feature and edition matrix.
  • Region count: Snowflake's Snowgrid blog cites 35+ regions across the three clouds. That figure is from the time of the post, so check the current region list, and confirm the specific Azure region you want.
  • Policies and tags: confirm masking and row access behavior on the secondary during validation.

8. Glossary

Organization
The umbrella over all of your Snowflake accounts. Required for cross-account replication.
Account
One Snowflake deployment in one cloud region.
Source and target
The account that holds the primary group (source) and the account that holds the replica (target).
Replication group
A bundle of objects copied from source to targets on a schedule.
Failover group
A replication group that can also be promoted so the target becomes the writable primary.
Refresh
One synchronization of a secondary group with its primary.
Promote (failover)
Make the secondary the new primary. Objects become writable there and read-only on the old primary.
Failback
Promote the original account to primary again.
Connection and Client Redirect
A named URL that resolves to whichever account holds the primary connection.
Listing
An offer of a data product (share or app package) to consumers.
Cross-Cloud Auto-Fulfillment
Snowflake automatically replicates a listing's data product to consumer regions.
Secure share area (SSA)
Snowflake-managed storage in each region that holds auto-fulfilled data products.
Tri-Secret Secure
Customer-managed key option that must be enabled separately on the target account.
