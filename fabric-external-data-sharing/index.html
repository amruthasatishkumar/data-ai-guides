External data sharing in Microsoft Fabric

Share OneLake data with another organization's Fabric tenant in place. Nothing is copied. The other tenant gets a read-only shortcut that points back to your live data, so they always see the current version without pipelines or exports.

No copies

The consumer gets a OneLake shortcut, not a duplicate. Changes at the source show up immediately.

Read-only

The consumer can query and analyze the data but cannot change it at the source.

Fabric to Fabric

Uses a dedicated cross-tenant mechanism. No Entra B2B guest accounts are needed.

How a share works, step by step

Fabric admins, both tenants

Turn it on. A Fabric administrator in the provider tenant enables External data sharing. A Fabric administrator in the consumer tenant enables Users can accept external data shares. Both are under export and sharing tenant settings, and each lets the admin scope which users can use it.

Provider user

Create the share. From the item's menu in the workspace or OneLake catalog, choose External data share, then pick the tables or folders.

Provider user

Invite. Enter the recipient's email to send an invitation, or copy the link and send it yourself. The recipient has 90 days to accept, after which the invitation expires.

Consumer user

Accept. Open the link, select Accept and select a location, pick a lakehouse you have write permission on, then a table or folder location. A shortcut to the shared data is created there.

Both sides can also be automated: the Fabric REST API includes External Data Shares Provider operations (create, list, get, revoke, delete) and External Data Shares Recipient operations (get invitation details, accept invitation).

Security: what you need to know before sharing

Your governance stops at your tenant boundary

  • Access is tenant-wide on the consumer side. The share grants read access to any user in the invited person's home tenant, not only the person you invited.
  • You cannot control who sees it there. The consumer can grant access to anyone, including guest users from outside their organization.
  • Purview protections do not follow the data. Sensitivity label visibility, downstream label inheritance, export encryption, and DLP are not enforced in the consumer tenant.
  • Data can cross regions. It might be transferred across geographic boundaries when accessed in the consumer tenant.
  • The consumer governs it from there. Once access is granted, the consumer tenant's own governance, access control, and compliance policies apply to the incoming share.
  • Your source policies still apply. Provider-side policies that restrict access to the data are enforced at the source and can prevent external consumption.

Revoking a share

Anyone in the provider tenant with Read and Reshare permission on the item can revoke a share at any time, from the External data shares tab on the item's manage permissions page. Revoking completely and irreversibly cuts off the consumer tenant's access, and anything they built on the shared data stops working. A revoked share can't be restored: you can create a new share, but the consumer has to rebuild their work from scratch. Agree on this with the consumer before revoking.

Limitations

  • Shortcuts inside a shared folder do not resolve in the consumer tenant.
  • Share links do not work for users in the provider's own tenant. Use regular OneLake shortcuts to share internally.
  • Consumers can only accept into a lakehouse, and need write permission on it.
  • Invitations not accepted within 90 days show as Expired and have to be sent again.
  • Revoking a share can't be undone.

Example scenarios

Illustrative examples of where the pattern can fit. They are not product claims or customer stories, so validate your own requirements and the security considerations above.

Public sector and education

Agencies or institutions share reference data across separate tenants without duplicating it.

Checklist before your first share

  • Both Fabric admins have enabled the right tenant setting and scoped who can use it.
  • You have Read and Reshare permission on the item, and it has at least one table or folder.
  • The data is approved for external sharing, given that labels and DLP do not carry across.
  • The consumer has a lakehouse ready to receive the shortcut, and write permission on it.
  • There is an agreed owner and process for revoking the share.
